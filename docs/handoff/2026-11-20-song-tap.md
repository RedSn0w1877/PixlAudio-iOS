# Song Tap, iOS, milestones 1 and 2 (2026-11-20)

Branch `song-tap` (from `origin/music-brain`). iOS port of Android's Song Tap (Android branch `song-tap` of `RedSn0w1877/PixlAudio`, handoff
`handoff/2026-11-20-song-tap.md` there). Milestone 1: the pure core with Android's golden files, the code route (QR), the receiver's
save and undo, the settings and a working Liquid Glass screen. Milestone 2 (the second half of this file): the Bluetooth tap, the beam
animation and the Developer mode diagnostics screen, and Android's spec version 2 (the Bluetooth wire format).

Song Tap sends the song that is playing to another phone with PixlAudio and adds it to that phone's library. Only a few ids and
the text travel (at most 400 bytes); each phone streams the audio itself. It works between any two phones (iPhone with iPhone,
Pixel with Pixel, iPhone with Pixel): the code is the same text on both.

## Behaviour

- Off by default. Turn it on from Settings › Song Tap or the "Turn on Song Tap" button on the screen. The player's ⋯ menu has a
  "Song Tap" row (On / Off) that opens the screen.
- Armed only while the screen is up and the app is in the foreground (scene `.active`): leaving the screen or the app disarms, stops
  the camera and cancels every timer. Nothing runs at app start; the controller is made on first use.
- Send: shows the playing song and a code. The code is remade every 4 minutes (it is good for 5). A song with neither a video nor
  a Spotify id says "This song can't be shared yet."
- Receive: the camera is asked for the first time the listener picks Receive and taps "Allow camera"; blocked says so and offers
  Open Settings. The first beam ever asks (Add / Not now); once the listener has said yes, later beams save at once and show Undo
  for 6 s. A song already in the library says so (no Undo). A song that cannot be found says "Couldn't find this one here" and
  saves nothing.
- Haptics: the engine's cues (tick on an offer, success on a save, warn on a failure) become `sensoryFeedback`, off while
  Haptic feedback is off.
- Private session needs no switch on the receiving side: the receiver reaches the library port only (a test pins the calls), never
  the listening history, the brain or the saved-during flag.

## Deviations from the research reports and from Android (the code won)

1. **Save path.** The brief named `SpotifyService.importYouTubeMusic`; on iOS that function writes the Spotify tables and nothing
   calls it. Search's Save (`YouTubeMusicSearchProvider.importAndPlay` and `like`) goes through `YouTubeLibraryImporter.importTrack`,
   which writes a `yt:<videoId>` row playable at once through `pixlstream://`. Song Tap uses that, with its own importer instance
   (same init, same library, same persistence). A Spotify-only song is matched with `TrackMatcher.findMatchFanOut` (25 s cap) over
   `env.spotify.bridge.search` and then saved under the video found.
2. **No register step.** `PlayableStore.register` / `BrainService.register` are not called: a `yt:` row plays without them, and
   registering would put a beamed song into the brain's recording tables.
3. **Undo** is `LibraryEditor.removeSongs` (the editor's name; `deleteSongs` is the persistence call under it). It also records the
   `yt:` id in `HiddenSongs`, which only the Spotify unified-library write reads, so beaming the same song again still works.
4. **"Already in your library"** is any of: the `yt:<videoId>` row, a Spotify row (`sp:<id>`) with that Spotify id, a Spotify row
   whose matched video is the beamed video.
5. **iOS songs from YouTube have no Spotify id**, so the sender names a song by its video alone when that is all it has
   (`AppSongTapSongIds.videoId(for:)`: the `yt:` song's own video, or the video matched to its Spotify id). Android requires a
   Spotify id (its YouTube songs carry a made-up one, which the sender drops: `isSyntheticId`).
6. **Undo of a song that was found by search** removes the video the save really used (the engine remembers it). Android's
   `startUndo` falls back to the Spotify id in that case, which cannot be removed; worth the same fix there.
7. **Scanning** is AVFoundation's QR detector, drawing is CoreImage (level L); Android uses zxing for both. Cross-reading both ways
   (Pixel scans iPhone's code, iPhone scans Pixel's) is the one thing in this milestone only two phones can prove.
8. **`SongTapQrTransport` has nothing to carry**: the camera view hands text to `SongTapEngine.scanned`, which decodes the link and
   keeps the same code from counting twice (Android splits the same work between `QrTransport.onScanned` and the state holder).
   The transport protocol is there for Bluetooth.
9. **Settings keys** live in `SongTapSettings.Keys`, not in `PreferenceKeys`: they are not Android preference keys and a backup does
   not carry them (Android's `song_tap` DataStore is not in its backup either).
10. **Type names** are prefixed `SongTap` (collisions: `SessionState`, and the short names beside it).
11. **The handshake `rttMs` for an ack** is 0 (as Android's `Ack`); the Bluetooth transport fills it in.

## How to test with two phones

Two phones with this build (iPhone and iPhone, or this and the Pixel once its build is on it). The code route needs no radio and no
internet for the code itself; saving needs the network (the song is matched to a video and its metadata is stored).

1. Both phones: Settings › Song Tap › Allow Song Tap (or open the screen and press Turn on Song Tap).
2. Phone A: play a song, open the player's ⋯ menu, tap Song Tap. Pick Send, tap Show code.
3. Phone B: open Song Tap, pick Receive, tap Allow camera (the system asks once), point it at A's screen.
4. Phone B, first time: a card asks Add / Not now. Tap Add: "Added to your library" with Undo. Find the song in the library and play it.
5. Beam a second song: it saves at once. Tap Undo within 6 seconds: it is gone from the library.
6. Beam the same song again after a minute: "Already in your library".
7. Beam twice within 3 seconds, or the same song twice within a minute: the card says why not.
8. Hold up a QR code that is not Song Tap's: "That isn't a PixlAudio song code."
9. Play a song that was never played before (a Spotify song with no video yet) and send it: the receiver searches and saves the match,
   or says it couldn't find it.
10. Cross-platform: do 2 to 4 with the Pixel as A and the iPhone as B, then the other way round.
11. Camera off for PixlAudio in Settings: Receive says so and "Open Settings" opens the app's page.

## The wire (the same bytes as Android, for the pair that matters most)

`SongTapWire` in `Packages/PixlCore/Sources/PixlLibrary/SongTap/` is a transliteration of Android's `SongTapWire.kt`; `Fixtures/songtap/wire.jsonl`
(from Android, byte for byte) is replayed by `SongTapGoldenTests.bluetoothWireFormat`.

| | UUID (lower case) | Properties | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| send service | `b7f3a1c0-5d2e-4c9a-8e61-3f4d7a2b9c01` | advertised by a sending phone | |
| receive service | `b7f3a1c0-5d2e-4c9a-8e61-3f4d7a2b9c02` | advertised by a receiving phone | empty, no server |
| Info | `b7f3a1c0-5d2e-4c9a-8e61-3f4d7a2b9c11` | read | `01`, age of the sender's last jerk in ms (u32 big endian, `FFFFFFFF` none) |
| Offer | `b7f3a1c0-5d2e-4c9a-8e61-3f4d7a2b9c12` | read | the signed frame (`SongTapSigning.encodeFrame`, at most 512 bytes); an ATT error until there is one |
| Control | `b7f3a1c0-5d2e-4c9a-8e61-3f4d7a2b9c13` | write (with response) | `01` Hello + 16 byte challenge, `02` Ack, `03` Decline, `04` Tap + age (u32) |
| Event | `b7f3a1c0-5d2e-4c9a-8e61-3f4d7a2b9c14` | notify | `01` Go |

- **Signature**: ECDSA P-256 over SHA-256 of `payload || challenge || timestamp (8 bytes, big endian)`, written as the raw 64 bytes **r then s** (not
  DER), CryptoKit's `P256.Signing.ECDSASignature.rawRepresentation`; the key is the 65 byte uncompressed point (`x963Representation`). That is the form
  Android's `SongTapSigner` writes and reads, and the frame fixes both lengths (`SongTapSigning.publicKeyBytes` 65, `signatureBytes` 64), so a frame from
  either platform verifies on the other. The goldens cover only the signed bytes, never a signature; a Pixel-to-iPhone verification is something only two
  phones prove. What a signature proves is tamper and replay (the receiver's own challenge and the time), not that the sender is honest.
- **Advertisement**: one 128-bit service UUID, nothing else (no local name, no data). A sender also runs the GATT server with the four characteristics;
  a receiver advertises its own UUID, serves nothing and connects, as the client, to the sender it is near. One connection, one direction.
- iOS peripherals can advertise only service UUIDs in the foreground, so everything else travels in GATT, which is how Android does it too.

## How a beam goes (as Android's, step for step)

1. Both phones are armed (the screen is open and the app is in the foreground). Each advertises its role's service UUID and scans for both.
2. Each follows the strongest other phone (another takes over only when 6 dB stronger and nothing is in flight). The approach glow is its smoothed
   signal. `peerFound`, `peerNear`, `peerFar` come from the detector.
3. A receiver connects to the sender it is near (smoothed above -70 dBm), discovers the service, turns on notifications for Event and writes Hello with
   a fresh 16 byte challenge.
4. The detector fires on a relative jump of 15 dB held 150 ms with a jerk on either phone within 400 ms, or the jump held 400 ms. The receiver reads
   Info (the sender's last-jerk age) when the jump starts. On a fire both phones announce the other's role, then the tap; a receiver also writes Tap.
5. Sender: the offer is published and signed on the read. Receiver: reads Offer, checks frame, payload, freshness (30 s) and signature, the session
   decides (first beam asks, rate limits), the song is saved, Ack is written. A new challenge goes out for the next beam whatever happened.
6. The sender answers Ack with Go; both animations start at T0 = go + 150 ms, the receiver's corrected by half the measured round trip of its Ack write.
7. The sender's beam is the whole send. The receiver's card rests six seconds with Undo, then slides away.

## The beam (Liquid Glass)

- **One clock, no animation state**: `SongTapTimeline.frame(role:tMs:reducedMotion:)` (the same permille numbers as Android) is read inside a
  `TimelineView(.animation)` that is *paused* unless a beam plays (so an idle screen draws nothing per frame); the frame is applied with scale, offset and
  opacity only. No blur, no per-frame allocation beyond the frame's own struct (`SongTapBeamFrame` is a value type, so Android's `frameInto` buffer is not
  needed).
- **Sender**: the artwork (248 pt, plain image) lifts with a shadow, shrinks to 40 percent and flies off through the top (the scroll view does not clip it),
  comes back, "Sent".
- **Receiver**: the artwork enters from the top at 40 percent and springs (the timeline's overshoot) into a Liquid Glass card in one
  `GlassEffectContainer`; the title, "Added to your library" and the Undo chip come in. The Undo chip is a separate glass capsule below the card that grows
  out of it (`glassEffectID` + `.matchedGeometry`), not a second glass layer inside it.
- **Glow**: one radial gradient at the top edge, strength = the larger of the approach and the beam's glow, plus a 0.6 s pulse at contact.
- **Reduce Motion**: the timeline's reduced frames (a 150 ms cross-fade, no flight, no pulse); the haptics stay (the session's tick, the beam's own cues).
- **Haptics**: the session's tick at contact and warning on failure; during a beam the timeline's cues (confirm, success), and the session's success is
  dropped so it never buzzes twice. Off when Haptic feedback is off.

## Deviations from Android and from the brief (milestone 2; milestone 1's are 1 to 11 above)

12. **The route is in PixlCore, not in the app.** Android's `BleTransport` is app code with a fake radio in its tests; here `SongTapBleRoute` is core code over
    the same seams, so 33 route tests and 13 two-phone tests run on this machine. The app has only `SongTapBluetoothRadio` and the sensor.
13. **Events reach the engine after the call that raised them**, in order (the route queues and drains), and **the engine runs each event to completion**
    (an event raised in the middle of another waits its turn). Android got the same from asynchronous flows; without it two linked phones in a test re-entered
    each other.
14. **iOS cannot disconnect a central** from the server side (`dropClient` does nothing; the receiver closes its own connection when the session ends) and
    **cannot choose the ATT error code** freely: "not ready" is 0x80 when `CBATTError.Code(rawValue: 0x80)` accepts it, else `unlikelyError`. The receiver
    treats any read error as "ask again", so both work; check on a device that a Pixel receiver retries against an iPhone sender.
15. **The radio answers `.active` at once and tells the truth later** (`onStatus`): CoreBluetooth reports its state a moment after the managers are made, and
    asks the system's Bluetooth question the first time they are made. So the question appears when the screen arms right after "Turn on Song Tap" (no setup
    page, no separate button). Denied: a card says so with "Open Settings" (iOS asks only once); off: a card says so; the code route works either way.
16. **The Undo chip is under the card, not inside it** (glass on glass), see above.
17. **The camera starts only on "Scan a code instead"** (milestone 1 started it when Receive was picked), as Android's screen now does.
18. **`frameInto` / `BeamFrameBuffer` are not ported**: a Swift struct needs no buffer.
19. **The diagnostics log's device line** is the model and system version (`UIDevice`), where Android writes manufacturer, model and API level.
20. **Two service UUIDs, one per role** (Android's deviation 11) and **the receiver is always the client** (its 12) are the wire; they are not iOS choices.

## How to test Bluetooth with two phones

Needs this build on an iPhone and either another iPhone or the Pixel with Android's `song-tap` build; Bluetooth on; Spotify signed in on both; a song playing on
the sender.

1. Both: Settings › Song Tap › Allow Song Tap (or the player's ⋯ menu › Song Tap › Turn on Song Tap). The first time, iOS asks for Bluetooth: Allow.
2. Developer mode on, both phones: Settings › Song Tap › Song Tap diagnostics. One on Send, one on Receive. Hold them 30 cm apart and press "30 cm", then 10, 3, 1
   and touching: Copy log has the numbers. Tap them together a few times and read "jump", "held for" and "last tap" on both. This is how the thresholds
   get calibrated (they are Android's guesses: `SongTapTuning`).
3. Phone A: play a song, Song Tap, Send. Phone B: Song Tap, Receive. Bring B's back to A's back slowly: the glow at the top of each screen grows and the line says
   "Phone nearby". Touch: both buzz once, the artwork leaves A through the top and B's card opens with the song and Undo. The song is in B's library and plays.
4. First beam on B asks Add / Not now. Not now: A says "Not added". Beam again: Add, and from then on it saves by itself.
5. Undo within six seconds removes it. The same song again within a minute says "You just got that one".
6. Both Send: both say "Both phones are sending. Switch one to Receive." Both Receive: nothing happens.
7. Turn Bluetooth off during a beam: both recover to waiting, the screen says Bluetooth is off. Deny Bluetooth: the card with "Open Settings".
8. Settings › Accessibility › Motion › Reduce Motion on: the beam cross-fades (no flight), the haptics stay.
9. Leave the screen or the app: scanning, advertising and the sensor stop (the diagnostics screen shows "idle").
10. Cross-platform, both ways (Pixel sends, iPhone receives; iPhone sends, Pixel receives): the pair that matters most, and the first thing to check.

## Not verified on a device

Nothing under `App/` has run. In particular: RSSI at contact on the iPhone and the Pixel 10 Pro and every threshold (a slow approach can read as a tap through
the jump-plus-dwell path: look at "jump" and "held for" first); how often CoreBluetooth reports a scan result with duplicates on (the detector was written for
about one a 100 ms); an iOS advertiser's interval against Android's scan; that Android finds an iOS advertiser by its service UUID filter; that the Android client
finds Event's descriptor on an iOS characteristic and negotiates down to iOS's smaller MTU, and that the long read of the signed frame (Read Blob) works in both
directions; that `CBATTError.Code(rawValue: 0x80)` is accepted (see 14); the notification (Go) when the Pixel is the receiver; the accelerometer's jerk against a
real tap; how the beam and the glow look and time out; the glass morph of the Undo chip; the haptic feel; first-frame decode of the artwork on the beam's screen.
